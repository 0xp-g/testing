The Self-Made Coder's Path

Your Personal Roadmap to LeetCode Knight

This guide contains every strategy, plan, and piece of advice we've discussed. It is your blueprint for success. Trust the process, trust yourself, and let's get to work.

1. The Grand Plan: Phase Breakdown

Your journey is a marathon, not a sprint. We've broken it down into three distinct phases, each with a clear goal and a specific set of topics from the TUF A-Z DSA Sheet.

Phase 1 Accelerated Foundations

Goal: Build a rock-solid foundation. Solve most Easy and many Medium problems.

  • Basics (Patterns, Hashing, Recursion)
  • Sorting & Binary Search
  • Arrays (All levels) & Strings
  • Linked List & Bit Manipulation
  • Recursion & Backtracking
  • Stacks, Queues & Two Pointers
  • Sliding Window & Heaps
  • Greedy Algorithms
  • Binary Trees & Binary Search Trees

Phase 2 The Grind & Advanced Topics

Goal: Master the gatekeeper topics. Push your rating towards 1800-2000+.

  • Graphs (Huge & Critical)
  • Dynamic Programming (Huge & Critical)
  • Tries
  • Advanced Strings (KMP, Z-function, etc. if time permits)

Phase 3 Peak Performance

Goal: Refine speed, accuracy, and strategy to break the 2200 barrier.

At this stage, your focus shifts from learning new topics to targeted revision. Use the TUF sheet to review and re-solve problems in your weakest areas identified during contests.

2. The Self-Made Coder's Schedule

Discipline and consistency are your greatest assets. This schedule is designed for your life, balancing your ambition with the need to avoid burnout.

Non-Gym Days ("Deep Dive")

Time: 2-3 hours.

Goal: Tackle new, challenging topics from the TUF sheet. This is for deep learning.

Gym Days ("Reinforcement")

Time: 60-90 minutes.

Goal: Solve 1 Medium problem on a topic you learned recently. This reinforces knowledge.

The Burnout Guard: Your Most Important Rule

Listen to your body and mind. It is NOT a failure to fall back to an easier plan on a tough day.

  • Feeling Strong? Execute the full plan.
  • Feeling Okay? Solve one Medium problem and call it a win.
  • Feeling Wiped Out? Solve one Easy problem. Just one. Consistency is the victory.

3. The Contest Playbook

Contests are your training ground. They are non-negotiable. Treat them as a skill to be practiced.

The 90-Minute Game Plan

  1. First 5 Mins (Reconnaissance): Open and read all 4 problems. Gauge difficulty. Don't solve yet.
  2. Next 20-25 Mins (The Sprint): Attack Q1 and Q2. Solve them quickly and cleanly.
  3. Next 30-40 Mins (The Core Battle): Focus on Q3. This is often the rank-defining problem.
  4. Remaining Time (The Moonshot): Attack Q4. Even a brute-force solution can score points.
  5. The 10-Minute Rule: If you make zero progress on a problem for 10 minutes, MOVE ON. Come back later.

The Upsolving Rule: Where Real Learning Happens

After every contest, dedicate a 2-hour block to upsolving. If you encounter a problem with an unknown concept:

  1. Identify the unknown pattern (e.g., "Trie", "DP on Trees").
  2. Do a "Micro-Learning" session: Watch the single TUF video that explains the concept for that specific problem.
  3. Solve the contest problem.
  4. Return to your main study plan the next day.

4. The Art of Problem Solving

This is the blueprint for how to think. Follow these steps for every problem you face.

  1. Absorb the Problem: Read it twice. Identify Inputs, Outputs, and especially CONSTRAINTS. The constraints are a massive hint about the required time complexity.
  2. Work Through Examples: Use the provided examples and create your own simple ones. Think of edge cases (empty arrays, duplicates, negative numbers).
  3. Visualize & Dry Run (The Whiteboard Phase): Use your physical notebook. Draw the data structures. Manually trace your logic with a pen. Draw arrays, pointers, trees, graphs. This catches most errors before you write a single line of code.
  4. Trigger Critical Thinking:
    • Always ask "Why?" Why does this approach work?
    • Generalize the problem. How would you solve it for K items instead of 3?
    • Embrace the struggle. Don't look at the solution for at least 30-45 minutes.

5. The Coder's Notebook

Your notebook is for ideas, not code. It's a "mental trigger" archive to train pattern recognition. Here's the format for each entry:

Problem: `15. 3Sum`

The Gist (1 Line):

Find all unique triplets in an array that sum to zero.


Key Insight / Pattern:

Sort the array. Then, for each element `i`, use a Two Pointer approach on the rest of the array to find pairs that sum to `-nums[i]`. (Pseudo-code is perfect here).


The Gotcha / Edge Case:

The main challenge is avoiding duplicate triplets. After finding a valid triplet, you must move the `left`/`right` pointers past any duplicate numbers.


Complexity:

Time: O(N^2), Space: O(1) or O(N)
